# The Score Machine — Super Sunday Presentation Framework

> **Product:** The Score Machine (thescoremachine.com) — AI credit analysis software, publicly positioned for **loan officers, credit pros, funding consultants and financial service providers**. Built by Ali (business partner: Dre / DeAndre Ruffin). Pulls a credit report, runs it through five AIs, and returns a red/yellow/green read on whether a file is fundable, what's blocking it, and what to fix.
> **Public feature names — use these on slides:** Full AI Credit File Analysis · **Underwriting Blueprint** · Progress Report + Score Timeline · Client Summary PDF · Credit Readiness.
> **Event:** Super Sunday — free live training. Funnel headline: *"Denied Again? The Bank Didn't Turn You Down For The Reason You Think."*
> **Scope:** credit and funding only. No material from any other business.

---

## 0. The One Thing This Deck Does

**Put the underwriting codex on screen and make the room read a real file with it.**

That is the entire event. Everything before it sets it up; everything after it sells the tool that does it automatically. On the Zoom training, the single strongest moment was Ali handing a volunteer the red/yellow/green codex and making *her* call the file line by line — she got to "Is this person fundable?" and answered "not quite" herself. Nobody argues with a conclusion they reached out loud.

Build the stage version of that moment and protect it. Cut anything that crowds it.

## 1. The Payoff Chain

The funnel makes four promises. Each has one mechanism from the software that pays it off:

| Funnel promise | The mechanism that proves it |
|---|---|
| "The bank didn't turn you down for the reason you think" | **Credit decay** — inquiries silently drop your approval ceiling below zero. *"Forget about points. It's about money."* |
| **#1 — THE APPROVAL GAP** | **The codex** — 700 score, still red across the board, still not fundable |
| **#2 — THE 90-DAY REBUILD** | **The paydown calculator** — utilization to target for $7–8 a week |
| **#3 — THE STACK** | **The file thresholds** — 5 primaries / 2 yrs history / 3 cards at $5K / 3 yrs age, then region-matched banks and products |
| "What another year of this actually costs you" | Their own denial, priced |
| "Let me be straight about what this is" | Honest frame, slide 8 |

## 2. The Underwriting Blueprint — the real scoring model

Publicly this is the **Underwriting Blueprint**. Use that name on every slide so what the room sees on stage matches what they see when they sign up; "the codex" is Ali's internal shorthand and works fine spoken.

Read exactly as Ali runs it. **Red = stop. Yellow = proceed with caution. Green = go.**

1. 700 credit score?
2. Under 30% utilization?
3. Minimum 5 open primary credit cards with 2 years of good payment history?
4. 3 primary credit cards with at least 3 years of age and a $5,000 limit?
5. More than 4 unsecured accounts opened in the last 12 months?
6. Under 4 inquiries?
7. Collections?
8. Charge-offs?
9. Late payments?
10. Bankruptcy?

**→ Is this person fundable?**

⚠️ **Line 5 needs a ruling before stage.** On the training it was read as a yes/no without a clear direction, and it's the one line an attendee can misapply on their own file. Lock whether "more than 4 in 12 months" scores red or green and say it that way every time.

Present this as *the Score Machine underwriting model* — not as a published bank policy. It's a conservative model on purpose, and that's a selling point: conservative estimates mean stronger files and fewer wasted applications.

## 5. The Audience Question — mostly settled

The product markets itself to **operators**: "AI Credit Analysis Software for Loan Officers & Credit Pros," built for credit consultants and funding teams. That is the buyer.

But the Super Sunday funnel is written in **consumer** voice — *you* got denied, *your* limit is stuck at $500. Both can be true, and it's a coherent play: hook on the pain they feel personally, teach the Blueprint on a real file, then show that the same tool that diagnoses their file is what the pros charge 10–20% to run. Consumer pain in, operator offer out.

What that means for the deck:

- **Acts 1–4 need no fork.** The Blueprint read works identically whether the file on screen is theirs or a client's.
- **Act 5 leads with the operator offer** — the tool, the classes, the affiliate program — because that's what the product sells and what the panel can support.
- **Give the personal-file path a real answer anyway.** Someone in that room only wants their own denial fixed. One slide routing them to the service side keeps them from feeling sold past.

Confirm the intended split with the panel before building Act 5. If the registrant list is overwhelmingly consumer, the honest move is to lead service and let operators self-identify in Q&A.

## 7. Live Demo Runbook

The training was derailed repeatedly by live pulls: wrong passwords, unpaid third-party accounts, a broken funding module, a dead purchase link, and the Zoom itself getting ended mid-demo. On a funnel-driven stage with a cold audience, that costs the room. Non-negotiables:

1. **No live credit pulls on stage.** Pre-pull both files — one clearly not fundable, one clearly fundable — hours ahead, and confirm both load.
2. **Screenshot everything.** Full-resolution captures of the codex read, the decay analysis, the paydown calculator, and the fundable file. If the platform hiccups, the deck carries the demo and nobody in the room knows the difference.
3. **Anonymize.** No real names, account numbers, addresses, or identifying detail on screen. Have permission for any real file shown.
4. **Do not demo a module known to be broken.** Screenshot it or skip it — "I broke it today" is fine in a paid customer class and expensive in front of cold registrants.
5. **Click the purchase link yourself** in the hour before going live, from a device that isn't logged in, and confirm what the page actually says. The training had a live buy link that read "start a free trial" when there was no free trial.
6. **Assign one person to the chat** for links, and post the link before it's asked for.
7. **Have the codex on a static slide** so the whole demo can be run without the software if it comes to that.

## 8. Compliance Guardrails

- **No guaranteed approvals, amounts, or score increases.** Funding numbers are estimates and must be said as estimates — the bank decides. This is already how Ali frames it; keep it that way on stage.
- **Every number on a slide is verified.** No invented case studies, no rounded-up results, no borrowed wins.
- **Real files only, anonymized, with permission.**
- **Nothing that reads as coaching anyone to misrepresent anything** to a lender or a bureau — not on a slide, not in an aside, not in Q&A.
- Slide 8 carries the honest frame in plain language, not buried fine print.
