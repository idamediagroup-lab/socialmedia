# The Score Machine — Presentation Framework

> **Product:** The Score Machine (thescoremachine.com) — AI credit analysis and funding-eligibility software.
> **Public feature names, use these on slides:** Full AI Credit File Analysis · **Underwriting Blueprint** · Credit Readiness · Progress Report + Score Timeline · Client Summary PDF.
> **Audience:** deliberately mixed and staying that way. Personal-credit attendees and business-funding attendees in the same room, every call going forward.

---

## 0. The Spine

**Nobody wants a credit score. They want what the score unlocks.**

The house. The car that gets them to work so they can provide for their family and be home more. The capital that lets the business take the contract instead of turning it down.

Every slide either names something they can't have yet, or moves them closer to having it. A deck about credit repair is boring. A deck about the thing on the other side of the approval is not.

**Say the score is a means, out loud, early.** It gives you permission to teach the technical middle of the deck without losing the room, because they already know what it's in service of.

---

## 1. The Logic Chain

This is what makes four separate features feel like one machine. Teach it in this order and every act earns the next:

> **You got denied.**
> → Not for the reason you think — inquiries quietly **decayed your ceiling**.
> → You got those inquiries because you **applied without knowing if you were eligible**.
> → The **Underwriting Blueprint** tells you eligibility *before* you apply.
> → If you're not eligible yet, the **letters and the paydown** fix the file — and the fix gets tracked.
> → Once you are, the **bank and bureau matching** tells you where to apply, which bureau they pull, and in what order.

Eligibility-first isn't caution. It's how you stop burning the ceiling you're trying to raise.

---

## 2. The Two Rooms — one deck, no fork

| | **Personal credit** | **Business funding** |
|---|---|---|
| What they want | The house. The car to get to work. Breathing room. | Capital to scale. |
| What's happening | Denied, no idea why. | Denied, no idea why — and no idea which bureau the bank even pulled. |
| What they're missing | Whether they should have applied at all | Which banks, which bureau, what order |

**Acts 1–5 serve both rooms with the same material.** A credit file is a credit file; the Blueprint reads it the same way whether the goal is a mortgage or a line of credit. Only the *examples* alternate — deliberately, so both rooms keep seeing themselves.

**Rule: never run two personal examples back to back.** Alternate. The moment one room goes three slides without seeing its own situation, you've lost it.

---

## 3. The Underwriting Blueprint — the scoring model

**Red = stop. Yellow = proceed with caution. Green = go.**

1. 700 credit score?
2. Under 30% utilization?
3. Minimum 5 open primary credit cards with 2 years of good payment history?
4. 3 primary credit cards with at least 3 years of age and a $5,000 limit?
5. More than 4 unsecured accounts opened in the last 12 months?
6. Under 4 inquiries?
7. Collections?
8. Charge-offs?
9. Late payments?
10. Bankruptcy?

**→ Are you eligible?**

⚠️ **Line 5 needs a ruling before stage.** Lock whether "more than 4 in 12 months" reads red or green, and say it the same way every time. It's the one line an attendee will misapply on their own file.

Present it as *the Score Machine underwriting model* — conservative on purpose. Conservative is the selling point: it protects the file from a wasted application.

### The ladder

Reveal one at a time, each striking through the last:

> ~~$997~~ → ~~$497~~ → ~~$247~~ → **$1 for 7 days** → then **$97/month**

⚠️ **Confirm the third rung.** It was given verbally as "twenty four ninety seven," which could be $247 or $24.97. $247 keeps the ladder descending cleanly into the $1 trial; $24.97 sits oddly below the $97/month it precedes. **Locked before this goes on a slide.**

### Offer rules

- **The ask is the trial, not the subscription.** $1 is a decision anyone can make from a stage. $97/month is a decision they make on day 6, after the Blueprint has already read their file.
- **Say the $97/month out loud.** Never bury the recurring price — a surprise on day 8 costs more in refunds and reputation than it ever gains in conversions.
- **Say "7 days" and the rebill date explicitly.** Both on the slide and in the room.
- ⚠️ **The public site sells per-pull and unlimited plans with a free no-card account.** This $1/7-day → $97/month structure is a different offer. **Confirm the checkout link actually charges $1 and rebills at $97**, from a logged-out device, before going live.

## 7. Live Demo Runbook

Live demos of this platform have failed repeatedly in the past — failed pulls, unpaid third-party accounts, broken modules, a dead purchase link. In front of a warm customer class that's survivable. In front of a cold room deciding on a $1 trial, it isn't.

1. **No live credit pulls on stage.** Pre-pull both files hours ahead — one clearly not eligible, one clearly green — and confirm both load.
2. **Screenshot everything** at full resolution: the Blueprint read, the decay figure, the paydown calculator, the bureau/bank list, the green file. If the platform hiccups, the deck carries the demo and the room never knows.
3. **Anonymize.** No real names, account numbers, or addresses on screen. Permission for any real file shown.
4. **Never demo a module you know is broken.** Screenshot it or cut it.
5. **Click the checkout yourself within the hour**, logged out, and confirm the price, the trial length, and the rebill terms.
6. **One person owns the chat** and posts the link before it's asked for.

## 8. Compliance Guardrails

This matters more with a personal-credit audience in the room than it did with an operator audience.

- **No guaranteed approvals, amounts, score increases, or removals.** Funding numbers are estimates; the bank decides. Removals depend on what's actually verifiable.
- **Be straight about what comes off a report and what doesn't.** Accurate negative items are not guaranteed removals. Slide 34 is where honesty buys you the sale.
- **Every number on a slide is verified.** No invented results, no rounded-up wins.
- **Real files only, anonymized, with permission.**
- **Nothing that coaches anyone to misrepresent anything** to a lender or a bureau — not on a slide, not in an aside, not in Q&A.
- Slide 10 carries the frame in plain language, not fine print.
