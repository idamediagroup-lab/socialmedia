## POST 1: Problem Diagnosis

Most credit clients don't quit because the work stopped working.

They quit because they stopped being able to see it.

The reason month-three retention drops is a problem with three things:

**Visibility** — they got one report at signup, then silence.
**Proof** — nothing shows what changed, only that another invoice cleared.
**Language** — the updates they do get read like a bureau wrote them.

Notice what isn't on that list: your actual work.

You can run a technically excellent file review and still lose the client, because the client is not evaluating your analysis. They can't. They're evaluating whether anything appears to be happening.

That's a reporting problem. Reporting problems are fixable.

A tracked score timeline — the same data points, plotted over time, in language a non-expert can follow — changes the monthly conversation from "am I paying for nothing?" to "okay, I can see where this is going."

Same file. Same work. Different retention.

That's why the progress report and the client summary are two separate outputs in Score Machine: the full analysis is for you, the timeline is for them.

What does your month-three retention actually look like right now?

## POST 2: The "PRO TIP" Post

PRO TIP: Stop reading credit reports front to back.

A consumer reads a report as a list of complaints — every negative line is something to attack.

An underwriter reads the same pages as a risk picture — what's recent, what's revolving, what's thin, what's already resolved.

Those two readings produce completely different advice.

The consumer reading produces a dispute list.

The underwriter reading produces a plan your client can sit across from a lender and defend.

If you want to be the person a client keeps *after* their first approval, do the second reading out loud. Walk them through their own file in the order a decision actually gets made in — what carries weight, what's noise, what genuinely deserves attention.

That's the line between selling disputes and being an advisor.

It's the same thinking behind the Underwriting Blueprint and the credit readiness view in Score Machine — one file, organized the way the decision-maker will read it.

Which reading are your clients getting from you right now?

## POST 3: State Your BEFORE and AFTER Transformation

Two versions of the same Tuesday.

**BEFORE**
Pull the report. Print it, or squint at 40 pages in a browser tab.
Highlighter for the derogatories, legal pad for the notes.
Re-type the important parts into an email so the client can follow it.
Do it again for the next client.
Bill for the strategy, spend the day on the transcription.

**AFTER**
Pull the report through the connected integration.
The file gets broken down and the patterns surfaced for you.
Generate the client-facing summary as a PDF.
Read it, correct it, send it.
Spend the time you got back on the conversation the client is actually paying for.

Nobody hires a credit consultant for their highlighting.

The judgment is yours — which accounts matter, what the plan is, what the client does next. That part doesn't get automated and shouldn't. The 40 pages of manual reading standing between you and using that judgment? That part should.

What's the piece of your week you'd hand over first?
